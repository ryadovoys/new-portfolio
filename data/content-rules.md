# Card Content Rules

Draft writing rules for `data/cards.json` descriptions.

## Why this exists

- Keep all cards in one clear voice.
- Make cards feel like portfolio content, not resume bullets.
- Keep claims specific and credible.

## Global rules (all tags)

- Write in plain, direct English.
- Prefer 2 sentences for `regular` cards.
- Use 3 sentences for `wide` cards when the project has meaningful scope.
- Keep one core idea per sentence.
- Use specific nouns (product, dashboard, design system, workflow) instead of vague words (things, stuff).
- Mention role/scope when useful, but avoid repeating the exact same phrasing across cards.
- Do not inflate impact or user numbers unless confirmed.
- Avoid filler phrases like "I love...", "explore new horizons...", "world-class...".

## Recommended length by card width

- `regular`: 24-45 words
- `wide`: 55-90 words
- `wide` + `Project`: 65-95 words (preferred when enough real detail exists)

## Tag-specific rules

## Personal

### Purpose
Establish identity and positioning in one quick read.

### Structure
1. Who you are now (role + breadth)
2. What makes your profile distinct (craft + systems + AI + leadership)

### Template
`Sergey Ryadovoy, [current role], with [X years] across [domains]. Strong in [core strengths] and known for [differentiator].`

### Avoid
- Long career timeline details
- Tool lists

## Project

### Purpose
Describe a concrete project and your role in it.

### Structure (required)
1. Start with what the project is
2. Then mention your role
3. Then mention scope/result

### Wide Project pattern (preferred)
Use a 3-sentence arc:
1. **Project context:** what the project is and where/when it happened
2. **Role and responsibility:** your title + what you led
3. **Delivery detail:** collaborators, outputs, and one concrete detail (count, system, flow, or artifact)

### Template
`A [type of product/work] for [audience/use case]. I was [role], leading [scope]. [Optional: key output or impact].`

### Wide Project template
`This project [what/where/when]. I was [role] on the team that [responsibility/scope]. In collaboration with [functions], we delivered [outputs] and [concrete detail].`

### Notes
- "Project-first" opening is preferred over "I..." opening.
- Keep it about the project narrative, not a resume summary.
- For wide project cards, keep useful detail (systems, collaborators, outputs) instead of over-compressing.

### Avoid
- Starting with role before describing the project
- Generic claims with no concrete scope

## Experience

### Purpose
Show ongoing domain experience or operating pattern across work.

### Structure
1. Define the domain or context
2. Explain how you operate in that space

### Template
`[Domain/context] has been a core part of my work. I apply it across [teams/products/workflows], focusing on [value created].`

### Avoid
- Listing random tools without context
- Turning it into one specific project card

## Skill

### Purpose
Show practical capability and how it is applied.

### Structure
1. Name the capability and where it is used
2. Mention tools/methods and output quality

### Template
`[Skill area] used across [project types]. I use [tools/methods] to deliver [clear output/outcome].`

### Avoid
- Hobby-style language ("I love...")
- Pure tool dumping without outcomes

## Experiment

### Purpose
Show exploration, curiosity, and hands-on making.

### Structure
1. What was being tested
2. What you built and what interaction/idea it explores

### Template
`An experiment exploring [question/hypothesis]. I built [artifact] to test [interaction/model/workflow] in practice.`

### Avoid
- Framing experiments as large-scale products
- Adoption claims unless verified

## Quality checklist before saving

- Does sentence 1 clearly answer "What is this?"
- For Project cards: is role mentioned after the project description?
- Is wording specific enough to visualize the work?
- Is grammar clean and concise?
- Is tone portfolio-first (not resume-first)?
- Are claims factual and defensible?

## Optional style consistency preferences

- Use first person in sentence 2 when mentioning role.
- Keep punctuation simple; avoid em-dash overuse.
- Use present tense for evergreen capabilities, past tense for completed work.
